## Fees and statements: what is in it

Home → **Fees and statements** (managers and the accountant, `finance.manage`). Three tabs, all about checking money that other companies take or report:

1. **Card fees.** Every card payment by card type (Visa, Mastercard, Debit/Interac, Amex, other) for a period, with the **estimated** processing fee from the rates in the setting `cards.fees` (for example Visa 1.6%, Debit 8¢ a payment). When the card deposit arrives in the bank and is matched (Bank), Chedam knows the **actual** fee for those days (sales less what was deposited); with the linked card processor (P5) it also gets the processor's own fee per payment. "Fees for the period" uses the actual fee where known, else the estimate, and goes into the profit and loss. Below it, the card processor's **payouts** (what Square paid into the bank and when).
2. **Platform payouts.** Uber Eats, DoorDash and SkipTheDishes send a payout statement (orders, commission, fees, adjustments, the amount paid). You type it in (and paste their order list if you have it); Chedam checks it against the delivery orders it recorded: orders missing on either side, different amounts, the commission %. It is "expected" until the bank shows that payout, then "in the bank".
3. **Vendor statements.** A vendor's monthly statement (what they think you owe, their invoices and credits): paste its lines and Chedam compares them with the bills in Chedam: agreed, different amounts, **missing in Chedam** (a bill you have not recorded), open in Chedam but not on their statement, and the difference in the balance. Each check is kept.

## Filling a vendor portal's cart by itself: why not, and what instead

Chedam will not sign in to a vendor's website and place items in its cart or submit orders by itself:

- Vendor portals (bottlers, distributors) have no ordering connection for a small store's software. Doing it anyway means keeping the store's **portal password on the hub** and having Chedam act like a person clicking through their pages. That breaks every time they change their website, and portals' terms of use usually forbid automated sign-in. A stored password for a supplier account is also a real risk.
- What is built does the thinking part (which vendor, how many, what it should cost, how to reach them) and saves typing: a draft purchase order (sent by email as a PDF from Purchase orders), or the **quick-order file** to upload on their portal, with the portal opened next to it. A person signs in (password manager) and presses order.
- **Real automatic ordering is possible where the vendor offers it:** EDI purchase orders (large suppliers, through an EDI service) or an ordering API (some distributors and B2B marketplaces). If Sreya lists the vendors the pilot store orders from most, Chedam can check which of them offer one and add it like the card processor: an adapter per vendor, outbound only, with the order still confirmed by a person.
